# Cerlock brand notes

## The name

Cerlock is Sherlock with the "Sh" swapped for the C in CS. The whole product comes from that one idea: a demo is a crime scene, every round leaves evidence, and Cerlock reads it and lays out the case.

## What it should not look like

Most CS tools look like one of two things. Esports overlays, with neon, angled shapes and heavy gradients. Or generic dashboards, with rounded cards in a grid and a blue accent. Cerlock should be neither. It should feel closer to a well kept case file: calm, precise and written by someone who knows the game.

## Principles

1. **Conclusions first, numbers second.** Say "deaths are not getting traded", then show the 13%.
2. **Every claim has evidence.** Anything Cerlock says links to the moment in the demo it came from.
3. **Quiet surfaces, loud evidence.** The interface stays dark and still, so the map, the mistakes and the moments stand out.

## Voice

Short sentences and plain words. Dry, never jokey. It should read like the notes of a coach who watched the demo twice. The detective theme shows up in a few names (Case files, Evidence, Ballistics), not in every sentence.

- Good: "Flashed a teammate, who died 0.8s later."
- Avoid: "Oops! Looks like someone forgot to communicate!"

## Logo

The mark is the C of Cerlock drawn as the lens of a magnifying glass. The handle comes out of the opening of the C, and a red dot sits where a crosshair would be. Detective, CS and the letter in one shape. The handle and the dot are the only red in the mark.

The wordmark is "cerlock" in Fraunces, lowercase, semi bold.

## Type

- **Fraunces** for display: map names, team names and headlines. A soft serif with a bit of old print in it.
- **IBM Plex Sans** for text. Clean and slightly mechanical.
- **IBM Plex Mono** for data: numbers, times and labels. Labels are set in small uppercase with wide spacing, like typed labels on a folder.

All three are open source (OFL) and bundled with the app, so it works offline.

## Shapes and details

- Hairlines instead of boxes. Sections are split by thin rules, not cards.
- Small radii, 3px at most. Only tags are rounded.
- **Evidence markers.** The numbered yellow tents used at crime scenes. Blunders are marked on the map with them.
- **Chalk marks.** Deaths are drawn as chalk crosses where the player fell.
- **The map** is lifted off the desk with a soft shadow, sits on a faint survey grid, and shows its own callout names, learned from the demo.
- **Player tokens** are drop shaped and point where the player looks. They are shaded like a small piece on a board, not flat dots.
